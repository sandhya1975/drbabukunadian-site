Angina & Coronary Heart Disease

Angina is pain or tightness, usually in your chest, that happens when your heart does not get enough blood — usually because fatty deposits have narrowed the arteries that supply it.

Book a consultation 📞 01244 680 444
Call 999 if chest pain does not ease after rest or medicine, you have sudden chest pain or pressure that does not go away, pain spreads to your arm, neck, jaw, shoulder, stomach or back, or you have chest pain with sweating, feeling sick, light-headedness or breathlessness. Do not drive yourself to A&E.

Recognising angina

  • Pain, tightness, squeezing, pressure or a dull ache in your chest
  • Pain that may also be felt in your neck, shoulders, jaw or arms
  • Sometimes feeling sick, breathless, dizzy or sweaty
  • Common triggers: exercise, stress, strong emotions, cold weather and large meals
  • Attacks usually last less than 10 minutes and ease with rest or medicine

If you have been diagnosed with angina

The NHS advises: if you get chest pain, take your prescribed angina medicine (usually GTN). If it has not helped after 5 minutes, take another dose. Call 999 if the pain has not eased 5 minutes after the second dose.

Treatment options

Treatment usually includes medicines to relieve attacks and reduce how often they happen, and medicines to lower your risk of heart attack and stroke. A procedure such as coronary angioplasty, or bypass surgery, may be recommended to improve blood flow to your heart.

Frequently asked questions

Is angina the same as a heart attack?
No. Angina happens when the heart does not get enough blood, and attacks usually ease with rest or medicine. A heart attack is a medical emergency — call 999 if chest pain does not go away.
Will I need a procedure?
Not always. Many people are treated with medicines. Coronary angioplasty or bypass surgery may be recommended to improve blood flow to the heart.
