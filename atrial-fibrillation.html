Atrial Fibrillation (AF)

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is a heart rhythm problem where your heartbeat is not steady. It raises the risk of blood clots and stroke, so expert assessment matters.

Book a consultation 📞 01244 680 444
Call 999 or go to A&E if your palpitations do not stop, or you also have chest pain, shortness of breath, or you feel faint or pass out. Do not drive yourself to A&E.

What is atrial fibrillation?

The NHS describes symptoms of AF as an irregular pulse, palpitations, a heart rate over 100 beats a minute, tiredness, chest tightness, breathlessness, and feeling light-headed or dizzy.

Some people have no symptoms at all, and AF is found during a routine check-up or tests for another problem.

AF raises the risk of blood clots and stroke. Anticoagulant (blood-thinning) medicines can lower this risk.

What an AF assessment covers

  • Confirming the diagnosis
  • Looking for related conditions, such as high blood pressure, an overactive thyroid or sleep apnoea
  • Assessing your stroke risk and whether anticoagulant medicine is right for you
  • Planning treatment with medicines to control your heart rate and rhythm
  • Ongoing follow-up and medication review

Frequently asked questions

Can AF cause a stroke?
AF raises the risk of blood clots and stroke. Anticoagulant medicines can lower this risk. Dr Kunadian will discuss whether they are right for you.
I have no symptoms — could I still have AF?
Yes. The NHS notes that some people with AF have no symptoms and are diagnosed during a routine check-up or tests for another problem.
Do I need a GP referral?
Yes. A GP referral letter is required for a private consultation.
