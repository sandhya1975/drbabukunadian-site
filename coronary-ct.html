Coronary CT (CT Coronary Angiogram)

A CT coronary angiogram is a specialised X-ray scan that produces detailed pictures of your heart and its blood vessels, showing any narrowing or blockages.

Book a consultation 📞 01244 680 444

Who is it for?

  • People with chest pain where coronary heart disease needs to be ruled in or out
  • People with risk factors or a family history of heart disease who need clear answers
  • Helping your cardiologist plan the right treatment

Before the scan

Let the team know if you have a contrast allergy, kidney problems, or might be pregnant. Bring a list of your medicines. You may be asked to avoid caffeine on the day, and not to eat for a short time beforehand. Your hospital will give you exact instructions.

During the scan

A thin tube (cannula) is placed in your arm so a contrast liquid can be given — you may feel warm and notice a metallic taste for about a minute. If clearer pictures are needed, you may be given medicine to slow your heart rate. The scan usually takes 10 to 30 minutes, and you should allow 1 to 2 hours in the department.

The scan uses a small dose of radiation, so it is only recommended when the benefits outweigh this small risk.

Specialist expertise

Dr Kunadian is accredited in Cardiac CT by the British Society of Cardiac Computed Tomography, so your scan is interpreted alongside your symptoms and history by a consultant cardiologist.

Frequently asked questions

Is a CT coronary angiogram painful?
No. Apart from the cannula in your arm, the scan is painless. You may feel warm and notice a metallic taste for about a minute when the contrast is given.
How long does it take?
The scan itself usually takes 10 to 30 minutes. Allow 1 to 2 hours in the department in total.
Is it covered by insurance?
Many private medical insurers cover it when it is clinically indicated. Please check with your insurer before your appointment.
