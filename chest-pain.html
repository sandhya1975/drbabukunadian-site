Chest Pain Assessment

Chest pain has many possible causes — including the heart, lungs, digestion, muscle strain and anxiety. A specialist cardiac assessment finds out whether your heart is involved, and what to do next.

Book a consultation 📞 01244 680 444
Call 999 if you get sudden pain or discomfort in your chest that does not go away, pain that spreads to your arm, neck, jaw, stomach or back, or chest pain with sweating, feeling sick, light-headedness or shortness of breath. Do not drive yourself to A&E.

Why get chest pain checked by a cardiologist?

The NHS lists several possible causes of chest pain, from heartburn and muscle strain to chest infections and anxiety. Some causes are heart-related, such as angina, which happens when the heart does not get enough blood — usually because of coronary heart disease.

A cardiology assessment gives you a clear answer, so you can either be reassured or start the right treatment early.

When to arrange an assessment

  • Chest pain that comes and goes
  • Chest pain that passes quickly, but you are still worried
  • Chest tightness or pressure brought on by exercise, stress, strong emotions or cold weather, which eases with rest
  • Chest discomfort and you have a family history of heart disease, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, diabetes or you smoke

Frequently asked questions

Is all chest pain heart-related?
No. According to the NHS, chest pain can also come from the lungs, digestion (such as heartburn), muscle strain, or anxiety and panic attacks. A cardiac assessment helps rule the heart in or out.
Do I need a GP referral?
Yes. A GP referral letter is required for a private consultation.
When should I call 999 instead?
The NHS advises calling 999 if you have sudden chest pain or discomfort that does not go away, pain that spreads to your arm, neck, jaw, stomach or back, or chest pain with sweating, feeling sick, light-headedness or shortness of breath.
